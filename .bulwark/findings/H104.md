## H104 — Server Clock Skew

**Verdict:** POTENTIAL

**Severity:** LOW

**Evidence:**
Authentication uses client timestamp with 5-minute window tolerance:

1. **auth.js:28**: `AUTH_TIMEOUT = 5 * 60 * 1000` (5 minutes)
2. **auth.js:75-88** (`verifyAuthMessage`):
   - Expected message: `"SolShot Auth: {wallet} at {timestamp}"`
   - Age check: `Date.now() - timestamp` must be in range `[-60000, AUTH_TIMEOUT]`
   - Allows 1 minute in future, 5 minutes in past

The tolerance is asymmetric:
- Forward skew: 1 minute allowed
- Backward skew: 5 minutes allowed

On devnet with high latency or clock drift, legitimate requests could fail. However, this is not a security vulnerability — it's a service availability issue. Server clock skew doesn't enable privilege escalation or financial loss.

The signature itself is on-chain verified (nacl), so an attacker cannot forge auth without the private key regardless of clock state.

**Recommendation:** Monitor for clock-drift failures; consider increasing forward tolerance to 2-3 minutes for unreliable networks.
