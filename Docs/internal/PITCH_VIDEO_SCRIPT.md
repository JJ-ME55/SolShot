# SolShot Pitch Video — 2-min Zoom Conversation (v2)

**Format:** Zoom recording, two webcams side-by-side (JJ + Fish), back-and-forth conversation, NO screenshare. 2-min hard cap, aim 1:55.

**Brief from Colosseum (verbatim):**
> "Separate from the demo video — introduce yourselves, tell us what you're building, and tell us why you're the people to build it. Nothing fancy required. **We're interested in how you think and communicate.** YouTube, Loom, or Vimeo. Up to 2 minutes."

**v2 reframe:** v1 was a polished one-person pitch with screenshare cuts. Wrong instrument. Colosseum is filtering OUT highly produced decks — they want to see two humans who know each other riff together. The Zoom-conversation format directly answers their "how you think and communicate" prompt. v1 archived in this commit's history (`git show HEAD~`).

**Audience:** Colosseum judges watching maybe 100+ of these. The format itself is the signal — produced one-person pitch reads as "founder doing pitch theatre." Two people on a Zoom call reads as "team that already works together."

**Status:** Draft v2, 2026-05-10. Recording target: Monday evening or Tuesday morning.

---

## What this video has to land

The Colosseum brief is asking three things, in this order:

1. **Who are you?** — names, what each of you brings
2. **What are you building?** — SolShot, in one breath
3. **Why are you the people to build this?** — the credibility / motivation / earned-the-right answer

The strategic moat material (multi-game, multi-platform, venture-scale tell) is *secondary* in this video — it's a tiebreaker bonus for judges who want more, not the headline. The headline is **the team itself.** Two people who can hold a 2-minute conversation about a thing they built together IS the pitch.

The demo video already proves *what-shipped*. The roadmap doc already proves *why-invest*. This video has one job: prove *who's doing it*.

## Conversation map (2:00 hard cap)

This is a beat sheet, not a script. The point of doing it as a Zoom call is that it doesn't sound scripted. **Don't write down sentences and read them.** Talk to each other like you do on a regular call.

### Beat 1 — Introductions (0:00 – 0:20)

**JJ leads.** Quick, direct, no preamble. Hit:
- Your name + handle (so it lines up with the GitHub commits judges will look at next)
- One sentence on what you do day-to-day on SolShot
- Hand off to Fish naturally ("…and Fish is on the call with me — Fish, do you want to introduce yourself?")

**Fish picks up.** Same shape:
- Name + handle
- One sentence on what you do day-to-day on SolShot — the product/design/strategy half
- Drop a hook into Beat 2 ("So what we're actually building…" / "JJ, want to give them the one-line on what SolShot is?")

### Beat 2 — What we're building (0:20 – 0:45)

**JJ on point.** The simplest possible version. No jargon, no "social-game layer for crypto group chats" framing on first pass. Try:
- "SolShot is artillery duels in your Telegram group chat. Two to ten people, you take turns over hours or days, you wager SOL, last tank standing takes the pot. Settled on-chain."
- One follow-on: it's live, you can play it right now at solshot.gg, the bot's `@SolShotGG_bot`.

**Fish adds the why-this-shape colour:**
- "The thing JJ is underselling is that nobody's done multiplayer wagered crypto inside a TG chat before. The async-turn cadence is what makes it actually fit — a real-time game would fight the chat for attention, an async one fits in next to the memes."

That's the wedge insight, delivered conversationally, not with a "Here's our wedge insight" sign hanging off it.

### Beat 3 — Why us (0:45 – 1:25) **← the heart of the video**

This is the longest beat and the one Colosseum is actually scoring. Both of you trade. **Don't take turns formally — interrupt each other.** That's the "how you think and communicate" the brief is asking about.

JJ's points to hit (in your own words, not these):
- I built SolShot solo from scratch in ~3 months, shipped from architecture through to live wagered matches on devnet
- I live in TG trench/whale chats, the audience for this product is people I talk to every day — I'm not designing for an imagined user
- I've shipped through three audits — on-chain, math invariants, off-chain — before mainnet, with full transparency in the repo

Fish's points to hit:
- I'm the product / strategy / design half — I came in when JJ already had a working core and we've been working closely since
- We've already shipped together through the hackathon push (GitHub commit history shows it — joint authorship)
- Specific things you brought: the design system, the litepaper structure, the strategic framing, *whatever you actually want to claim — don't oversell, judges will check*

The "why us" answer that lands: **we're the people who've already been building this together for months. The team isn't a hypothetical — go look at the commit history.**

If you want a single line that ties the beat: *"We're not pitching what we'd build if you funded us. We're pitching what we already built."*

### Beat 4 — Where this goes (1:25 – 1:50)

Whoever's natural lead here goes. The vision beat — short, high-altitude, no listing.

The frame to land (paraphrase, don't recite):
- "The artillery game is the wedge. The bet is that group-chat-native gaming becomes a category — across Telegram first, then Seekr Mobile, then iMessage and WhatsApp. Multiple games on the same backend, same SHOT economy. Nobody's built this. We think we can."

If Fish leads this beat, JJ chimes in with a single anchoring point:
- "Yeah and the architecture is already set up for that — same Anchor programs, same wallet layer, the chat shells are basically frontends to one backend. Adding a surface or a game isn't a rewrite, it's an addition."

That's the venture-scale tell delivered conversationally. **Don't quote-deliver the formal line.** The strength of the Zoom format is that the same thought lands more credibly when it sounds like something one of you would actually say.

### Beat 5 — Close (1:50 – 2:00)

Whoever's been on point most recently lands the close. Direct, short, friendly.

- "Mainnet's a week or two out. We're applying because we want to skip the part where we slow down to fundraise. SolShot.gg, code's open on GitHub. Thanks for watching."

Done. Hard out.

## Production notes

- **Setup:** Zoom call, gallery view ON so both webcams show side-by-side. Record locally if your bandwidth is iffy — gallery-view recording from cloud Zoom can be lower quality than local. Loom also works, both webcams active.
- **Lighting:** face a window or a lamp. Don't backlight yourselves. Do this 5 minutes before recording, not at recording time.
- **Audio:** wired headphones with mic > AirPods > laptop mic. Test before. Bad audio kills these videos faster than bad video.
- **Don't sit silent while the other talks.** Nod, "yeah", smile. Static-listening face for 30s on camera reads as awkward. Stay alive.
- **Take it twice.** First take is for warm-up, second take is the keeper. Don't fall in love with take 1.
- **Hard 2-min cap.** Edit out any silence > 1 second. Quicktime / Loom / Riverside all let you trim. Don't add B-roll or music — Colosseum said "nothing fancy required." Polish hurts here.
- **Background:** plain wall or a non-distracting room. No bookshelf-flexing, no "look how cool my home office is" lighting, no logos on display.

---

## What NOT to do

- **No screenshare.** This isn't a demo. The product proof is in the demo video.
- **No formal pitch deck open behind you.** Colosseum is explicitly filtering this out.
- **No memorised speech.** If you're reading from a script you'll sound like it. Use this doc as a memory jog 10 minutes before recording, then close it.
- **No "as a founder…" / "we strongly believe…" / VC-deck phrasing.** Talk like two friends explaining a project to a smart person who isn't deep in crypto.
- **No buzzword stack.** Don't say "category-defining," "TAM," "go-to-market," "primitives layer." Use plain words. The strategic framing comes through *anyway*.

---

## If you only remember three things

1. **The format is the message.** A 2-min Zoom conversation is itself proof you work together. The content matters less than the dynamic.
2. **Why us is the longest beat.** "We're not pitching what we'd build if you funded us — we're pitching what we already built."
3. **Land Beat 4's wedge insight conversationally**, not as a quote. If it sounds rehearsed it loses force. The natural delivery is the credibility.
